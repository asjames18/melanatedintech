## Direct answer

Churches can use AI responsibly for low-risk support work such as drafting public announcements, organizing approved nonconfidential notes, preparing checklists, and creating volunteer-role materials. AI should not replace pastoral presence, spiritual discernment, safeguarding, counseling, or consequential decisions about people or money. Start with fictional or public data, require a named human reviewer, document what the tool may access and do, and test the workflow before connecting church systems.

## Accuracy and trust requirements

- Do not imply universal legal compliance or give legal advice.
- Do not recommend putting prayer requests, counseling notes, giving details, child information, or other sensitive records into a general AI tool.
- Do not claim that volunteer scheduling is automatically low risk; distinguish draft/checklist support from assignments involving minors, transport, safeguarding, or credentials.
- Keep pastoral care, spiritual discernment, safeguarding, discipline, hiring, financial approvals, and other consequential decisions human-owned.
- Treat provider features and data terms as plan-specific and changing; require current vendor review.
- Label on-site agents and products according to their actual delivery and setup requirements.
